00 / Statement Artist Statement

A grid is not a picture of order — it is a way to organise chaos.

I am an Iranian-born artist working at the intersection of classical textile logic and generative systems. My work begins with a simple observation: the woven surface — repetitive, mathematically structured, infinitely variable — has always been a form of computation, long before computers.

Gradient Grid takes the grid as a field of values. Every cell carries a seeded number between −450 and 450, and that number is mapped to a position along a linear gradient running between two colours drawn from a palette of sixteen. The grid itself is invisible; only the gradient remains.

What emerges is a field of light — a surface that reads as fabric, screen, weather map, or terrain depending on the palette the seed chooses. Each piece is defined by a numeric seed and can be regenerated, documented, and adapted for textile, print, and surface applications.

On AI tools — where I encountered technical obstacles, I turned to AI tools for debugging and code optimization. Every structural, aesthetic, and conceptual decision remained my own.

Structure

01 / Seed

Variation

A numeric seed picks two colours from a palette of sixteen, the grid dimensions (10–159 columns), and every cell's value (−450 to 450). Each output is regenerated and documented as a distinct, traceable work.

02 / Mapping

Geometry

Every cell's value is normalised to 0–1 and used to interpolate between the two palette colours. The cell is then filled with the interpolated colour. The grid is invisible — only the gradient field remains.

03 / Surface

Application

The resulting composition becomes a surface-design asset — explored across garments, textile prints, wallpaper and other pattern-driven applications.
